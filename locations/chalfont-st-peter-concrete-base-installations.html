Ground preparation

Why Proper Ground Preparation Matters

The concrete itself is only part of the installation. Before the pour, the ground underneath needs to be suitable for the intended use and prepared correctly. Cutting corners at this stage can leave the finished base vulnerable to movement later.

One of the problems we avoid is a poorly prepared sub-base allowing the concrete to settle unevenly. Once an outdoor building has been installed, correcting movement underneath it becomes a much bigger job.

Our approach is to deal with the ground properly before concrete arrives. We excavate the required area, prepare the sub-base, set the formwork and check the levels before the concrete is placed. The installation is then finished as a clean, level platform for the intended building.

This is particularly important where the homeowner is investing in a permanent outdoor structure and wants the base to remain suitable for it over time.

Organised installation

A Practical Installation Process in Chalfont St Peter

We keep the installation straightforward and organised. Before starting, we establish the required footprint and look at how the working area can be accessed. This is especially useful on larger residential properties where there may be several possible routes into the garden.

The working area is then marked out and excavated to the required depth. The ground is prepared and compacted before the base is formed, with levels checked so the finished concrete sits correctly within the planned position.

Once everything is ready, the concrete is placed and finished. We keep the work area controlled throughout the installation and avoid unnecessary disturbance to surrounding parts of the garden.

For homeowners arranging a new outdoor building, this means the base is dealt with as a separate stage of the project and left ready for the next part of the installation.

What we look at before starting

  • The size and position of the proposed base
  • Ground conditions across the working area
  • How materials and equipment can reach the garden
  • Existing paving, planting and surrounding areas
  • The finished level required for the intended building
