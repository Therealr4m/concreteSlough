Pro Concrete Bases On Uneven Ground In Slough

A Concrete Base Starts With The Ground Underneath It

If the garden where your shed is going is uneven, sloping or has been disturbed before, we do not simply pour concrete over the existing surface and hope for the best. The preparation underneath the slab is what determines how well the finished base performs.

We install concrete bases in Slough by dealing with the levels first. That can mean excavating high areas, removing unsuitable material, building up lower sections and installing a properly compacted sub-base before the concrete is placed.

For a small garden shed, the finished base needs to give the building a genuinely stable and level surface. If the ground underneath is left uneven or poorly compacted, the problem can eventually show itself in the structure sitting on top.

Our approach is straightforward: understand the levels, prepare the ground properly, organise the access and concrete delivery, then install the slab to the required dimensions and finish.

Uneven ground preparation

What We Actually Do When The Garden Is Uneven

Uneven ground does not automatically mean the job is complicated. What matters is understanding why the levels are uneven and preparing a suitable formation for the concrete.

On a typical Slough garden shed project, we establish the required finished level first. We then work backwards from that level to determine how much excavation, build-up and sub-base preparation is required.

The groundwork can include

  • Setting out the correct footprint and finished levels
  • Excavating high or unsuitable areas of the existing ground
  • Removing loose, disturbed or unsuitable material where necessary
  • Building up lower areas with suitable compacted sub-base material
  • Levelling and compacting the formation before concrete arrives
  • Installing suitable edge formwork to contain the concrete
  • Considering drainage and finished levels so water is not directed towards the structure
  • Using reinforcement where the project and ground conditions make it appropriate

The exact preparation is decided from the actual ground and proposed building rather than applying the same specification to every garden.

Common Slough garden situations

When An Uneven Garden Needs More Than A Simple Pour

We regularly come across gardens where the area selected for a shed has never been properly prepared for a building. Sometimes it is an old lawn, sometimes an area that has previously been excavated, and sometimes the garden simply has different levels across its width.

A Shed On A Sloping Garden

Where the garden falls away, we establish a suitable finished level for the shed and prepare the ground to reach it. Depending on the difference in levels, that can involve excavation at the high side and additional sub-base preparation at the low side.

Previously Disturbed Ground

If the area has previously contained paving, an old structure, a removed patio or another excavated feature, the ground can contain a mixture of materials that does not compact evenly. We assess what is actually there rather than assuming the existing surface is suitable.

A Shed Replacing An Older Structure

Removing an old shed does not necessarily leave behind a suitable foundation for the replacement. Old slabs, loose fill and partially disturbed ground may need to be removed or reworked before we establish the new base.

Different Levels Across The Garden

If one side of the proposed base sits higher than the other, we plan the excavation and build-up around the finished level. This is particularly important where the shed needs a clean, level installation rather than simply following the existing garden slope.

Practical site considerations

Access, Drainage And Concrete Supply In Slough Gardens

Uneven ground often goes hand in hand with awkward garden access. Before starting, we consider how excavation material will be removed, how sub-base material will get to the work area and how the concrete will reach the base.

A narrow side passage, steps, gates or changes in garden level can all affect how we organise the job. Working with restricted access usually just means planning material movement differently and keeping the installation organised from the beginning.

Drainage also needs to be considered. We do not want to create a situation where the finished base encourages rainwater to sit against the shed or garden building. Finished levels and any appropriate fall are considered during preparation and setting out.

Concrete delivery is planned around the site

Once the groundwork is ready, the concrete is arranged through established batching plant supply. We plan the delivery around the access available and the amount of concrete required so the pour can be handled efficiently once the load arrives.

The important part is having the groundwork ready before the concrete turns up. There is no benefit in having a concrete load waiting while levels, formwork or sub-base preparation are still being sorted.

How we install the base

Our Uneven Ground Concrete Base Installation Process

1. Establish the building footprint

We start with the dimensions and position of the shed. This gives us the footprint we need to work from and allows the finished concrete level to be planned around the building.

2. Check the existing levels

We look at the difference between the high and low points and work out how the existing ground needs to change. This is where we identify whether excavation, additional sub-base or a combination of both is needed.

3. Excavate and prepare the formation

High areas are excavated as required and unsuitable material is removed. Lower areas can then be built up using appropriate sub-base material, placed and compacted properly rather than simply spreading loose material underneath the slab.

4. Set the formwork and final levels

Once the formation is prepared, we set the edges and check the finished level. This is the stage where we make sure the base will finish where it needs to be rather than discovering a level problem after the concrete has been poured.

5. Prepare reinforcement where required

For a small garden shed, reinforcement requirements depend on the particular base and ground conditions. Where reinforcement is appropriate, it is positioned as part of the preparation rather than treated as an afterthought.

6. Place and finish the concrete

Once everything underneath is ready, the concrete is placed, levelled and finished to provide the required surface for the shed. The delivery and finishing work are coordinated so the concrete can be handled while it is workable.

7. Final checks and clean-up

We check the finished levels and edges, tidy the working area and leave the base ready for the next stage of the shed installation.

Why getting the levels right makes all the difference

The Concrete Is Only As Good As The Preparation Underneath It

This is the part we put the most attention into on uneven-ground jobs. A strong concrete mix does not compensate for a poor formation underneath it.

If one section of the ground is loose while another is properly compacted, the two areas can behave differently under load. That is why we prepare the whole footprint rather than concentrating only on making the visible surface look level.

Getting the levels right also makes the shed installation easier. The building can be positioned on a proper, consistent surface instead of having to compensate for an uneven foundation.

  • More consistent support beneath the building
  • Better control of the finished shed position
  • Reduced risk of movement caused by poorly prepared ground
  • Proper consideration of water run-off and surrounding levels
  • A cleaner foundation for the shed installer to work from
What can go wrong

An Uneven Base Can Affect The Building Above It

One of the common mistakes we see is treating the concrete slab as though making the top surface level is the whole job. If the ground underneath has not been prepared properly, the finished base can still have problems later.

A shed installed on a base with inconsistent support can develop movement or become difficult to keep properly aligned. Doors can stop operating cleanly, gaps can appear and the structure can end up sitting differently from how it was intended.

Poorly planned finished levels can also create drainage problems around the building. Water sitting against the structure or being directed towards the base is something we aim to avoid during the initial setting-out stage.

The problems usually start underneath

  • Loose or poorly compacted ground can settle unevenly
  • Different ground levels can leave the structure improperly supported
  • Incorrect finished levels can create water-management problems
  • An incorrectly sized or positioned base can make shed installation difficult
  • Trying to correct poor preparation after the concrete is down is far harder than doing it properly beforehand

That is why we would rather spend the necessary time getting the groundwork and levels right before the pour than try to make up for poor preparation once the concrete is already down.

Before we quote the work

What We Look At On An Uneven Ground Job

When arranging an uneven-ground concrete base, we want to understand the job before the installation date. The shed dimensions, proposed position, ground levels and access all affect how the work is organised.

We consider

  • The size and footprint of the proposed shed
  • How much the existing garden levels change across the base
  • Whether the ground has previously been disturbed
  • How excavation material can be removed
  • How sub-base and concrete can reach the installation area
  • The required finished level of the shed
  • Drainage and surrounding garden levels
  • Whether reinforcement is appropriate for the particular project

This gives us a practical basis for pricing and scheduling the work rather than guessing from the concrete area alone.
