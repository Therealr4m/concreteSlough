What we assess first

Every Garden Needs a Slightly Different Installation Approach

There is rarely much value in treating every garden base exactly the same. Two sheds of the same size can require different preparation depending on what is underneath them and how the garden is laid out.

With an uneven or previously disturbed garden, we look at how much material needs to come out, where the finished base needs to sit and whether the existing ground is suitable to build from.

We also check how water is likely to move around the proposed base. The finished installation needs to work with the garden rather than creating a low point where rainwater can collect against the building.

Ground preparation

Where necessary, we excavate the footprint down to suitable ground and remove loose or unsuitable material. We then build the base back up with a properly compacted sub-base rather than relying on the existing garden soil to carry the load.

On previously disturbed ground, this preparation is particularly important because buried material, old landscaping or loose fill can behave differently once the new building is placed above it.

Finished levels and drainage

Before concrete is placed, we establish the required finished level and make sure the base is positioned correctly in relation to the surrounding garden.

Where the site requires it, we account for drainage and suitable fall so surface water does not simply sit against the concrete or building. The exact approach depends on the garden and how the shed will be positioned.

Access and concrete logistics

Most Slough garden projects have some form of access constraint, even where access is considered reasonably straightforward. We look at the route from the delivery point to the garden before the work starts so excavation material, sub-base and concrete can be moved efficiently.

For larger pours or restricted access, concrete supply can be arranged around the site conditions. Depending on the job, this may involve ready-mix delivery or other suitable concrete supply arrangements rather than trying to handle the pour inefficiently on site.

How we install it

A Bespoke Concrete Base Built From the Ground Up

Once the footprint and requirements are agreed, we set out the base accurately before starting the physical preparation. This gives us a clear working area and helps make sure the finished concrete ends up where the garden building actually needs it.

1. Set out the building footprint

We establish the required dimensions and position of the base, allowing for the actual shed or garden building rather than guessing the footprint from the available garden space.

2. Excavate and prepare the ground

Uneven ground is brought under control by excavating the required area and removing loose or unsuitable material. Where the existing ground has previously been disturbed, we take particular care not to build directly onto material that may settle.

3. Install and compact the sub-base

A suitable sub-base is placed and compacted in preparation for the concrete. This provides a more stable working platform and helps distribute the load rather than leaving the concrete slab dependent on soft garden soil.

4. Form and establish the levels

Formwork is installed to contain the concrete and establish the required dimensions. We check levels at this stage because correcting the position or level after the concrete has been poured is obviously not an option.

5. Reinforce where the job requires it

Reinforcement is considered where the design, dimensions, ground conditions or expected loading make it appropriate. We do not automatically add reinforcement to every small shed base simply for the sake of it; it is selected around the actual requirements of the installation.

6. Arrange and place the concrete

The concrete supply is organised around the quantity required and the access available. Once the pour starts, we place and work the concrete into the prepared formwork, keeping the finished level and surface suitable for the building.

7. Finish and check the completed base

The surface is finished and the base is checked before the installation is considered complete. We want the finished platform to be properly positioned, stable and ready for the next stage of the garden building installation.

Long-term performance

The Concrete Base Should Suit the Building Above It

A shed base is not just a piece of concrete put somewhere in the garden. Its dimensions, position, level and preparation all need to work with the structure that will eventually sit on it.

Getting this right from the beginning means the shed has a proper foundation rather than being placed onto a surface that can move, settle or hold water.

We also avoid creating unnecessary problems for the garden around it. The base is positioned and prepared with the surrounding levels and drainage in mind, so the finished installation works as part of the space rather than becoming an obstruction.

Common installation mistakes

When the Base Does Not Match the Building Requirements

One of the most common problems we come across is a base being installed to a convenient size rather than to the actual requirements of the building.

If the footprint is wrong, the shed may not sit correctly on the concrete. Edges can be left unsupported, doors can be affected by poor levels and the final position may not work with the surrounding garden.

Poor ground preparation creates a different problem. If loose or disturbed material is left underneath the slab, sections of the base can settle differently over time. That can leave the building uneven even though the concrete initially looked fine.

Drainage is another part that needs considering before the pour. A base positioned without thinking about surrounding levels can interfere with water movement and leave moisture collecting around the building.

Problems we are trying to prevent

  • Uneven settlement caused by inadequate ground preparation
  • A shed footprint that does not properly match the concrete base
  • Unnecessary cracking caused by unsuitable preparation or installation
  • Water collecting around the base because surrounding levels were ignored
  • Difficulty positioning or installing the garden building correctly

It is usually far easier and cheaper to get the footprint, preparation and levels right before the concrete goes down than to correct the installation afterwards.
