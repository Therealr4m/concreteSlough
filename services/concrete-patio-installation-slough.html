Groundwork and patio preparation

Preparing Your Garden For A New Concrete Patio

On a typical Slough patio installation, we start by marking out the new area and checking the levels against the house, garden boundaries and surrounding paths. We need enough depth for the compacted sub-base and concrete without leaving the finished patio too high against the property.

If the garden has old paving, we lift the slabs and inspect what's underneath. Some patios have little more than loose sand and soil below them. Others have sections of old concrete mixed with hardcore. We don't simply cover unstable material and hope it holds.

Where the existing base is unsuitable, we break out and remove the affected sections, excavate to the required formation level and prepare the ground for a new compacted sub-base.

What We Check Before Concrete Goes Down

  • Existing paving, concrete remnants and unsuitable ground beneath the patio
  • Required excavation depth and the condition of the exposed subgrade
  • Finished patio height relative to doors, paths and damp-proof course levels
  • Drainage direction and the fall needed to move surface water away from the property
  • Access for removing spoil and bringing concrete into the garden
  • Concrete thickness, edge support and reinforcement requirements for the intended use

We also check for visible drainage covers, inspection chambers and services that could affect the layout. Any areas needing access are planned around rather than permanently covered without consideration.

Proper excavation and sub-base preparation are particularly important when replacing an older patio that has already started sinking or moving.

Our installation process

How We Install Concrete Patios In Slough

We organise patio installations around the groundwork, access and concrete delivery requirements. Getting these stages in the right order keeps the job moving and avoids unnecessary disruption once the concrete is ready to be placed.

1. Site Visit And Patio Measurements

We inspect the proposed patio area, take measurements and discuss how the space will be used. We check existing ground levels, access routes and any old paving or concrete that needs removing. From there, we can establish the preparation and concrete work required.

2. Excavation And Removal Of Existing Materials

We lift existing slabs, break out unsuitable concrete where necessary and excavate the patio footprint to the required depth. Spoil and broken materials are moved out through the agreed access route, with surrounding paths and garden features considered during the work.

3. Sub-Base Installation And Compaction

Once the ground is prepared, we install suitable granular sub-base material and compact it in layers. We check the formation and levels as we work, dealing with any soft or unstable areas before the concrete stage.

4. Formwork, Levels And Reinforcement

We set secure formwork to the patio dimensions and establish the finished levels and drainage fall. Where reinforcement is specified, we position it with suitable supports so it sits within the concrete at the intended depth rather than directly on the ground.

5. Concrete Delivery And Placement

We arrange the concrete supply around the volume required and the available access. Depending on the job, concrete may be supplied ready-mixed from a batching plant or prepared using another suitable method. We organise the movement and placement so the slab can be poured and worked without unnecessary delays.

6. Finishing, Jointing And Curing

We level and finish the concrete to suit its intended use, maintaining the required drainage fall. Movement-control joints are planned where appropriate, and the fresh concrete is protected while it cures. We explain when the surface can be accessed and when it will be suitable for its intended loading or further work.

The preparation, thickness, reinforcement and finishing method are agreed around the individual patio rather than using one specification for every garden.

Garden access and site organisation

Concrete Patio Installation With Typical Garden Access

Many patio installations in Slough involve working through a side passage, shared access route or directly through an existing garden. We plan how materials will be brought in and how excavated soil and broken paving will be removed before starting the main groundwork.

Where wheelbarrow access is suitable, we organise material movement around the available route. If the concrete delivery vehicle cannot reach the garden, we assess whether barrowing, pumping or another practical placement method is appropriate.

We also consider existing lawns, fences, steps and neighbouring boundaries. The working area needs enough room for excavation, formwork and concrete finishing without unnecessarily disturbing the rest of the garden.

Common Access Considerations

  • Side gates and narrow passages between properties
  • Existing paving that needs protecting during material movement
  • Steps or changes in level between the front and rear garden
  • Limited space for storing excavated soil and sub-base materials
  • Positioning concrete deliveries without blocking access unnecessarily

We work out these practical details at the site visit so the installation can be organised around the property rather than discovering access problems on pouring day.

Long-term patio performance

Concrete Patios Prepared To Resist Movement And Settlement

A concrete patio needs support across its full footprint. If one section is laid over compacted hardcore and another sits over loose, disturbed soil, the ground can settle differently beneath the slab. That's when cracking, uneven edges and movement can start becoming noticeable.

We pay particular attention to areas where old patios have been extended or partially removed. These sections often contain different materials underneath, so we check the exposed ground and prepare it consistently before installing the new concrete.

Ground Support And Concrete Thickness

We determine the slab construction from the ground conditions and intended use. A standard pedestrian patio has different loading requirements from an area supporting a heavy masonry feature. We account for that before setting the formwork or ordering concrete.

Drainage And Surface Falls

We establish a suitable fall so rainwater moves towards an appropriate drainage point or outlet rather than collecting against the property. We also consider existing garden drainage, surrounding ground levels and the position of doors and air bricks.

Reinforcement And Movement Control

Reinforcement can help control crack widths and improve slab performance where specified, but it doesn't replace good ground preparation. We also consider joints and the connection to adjoining surfaces so normal concrete movement is managed as part of the installation.

Concrete naturally shrinks as it cures, and no installer can guarantee a completely crack-free slab. Our approach is to reduce avoidable problems through sound preparation, appropriate jointing, controlled placement and proper curing.

A well-built patio starts below the finished surface. The concrete can only perform as intended when the supporting ground and drainage have been properly considered.

Avoiding installation problems

Why Poorly Installed Concrete Patios Crack

We regularly assess the same underlying issues when looking at older concrete surfaces: insufficient excavation, poorly compacted ground, inconsistent slab thickness and inadequate consideration of drainage or movement.

A patio can look perfectly acceptable when the concrete is first finished. Problems often become apparent later, particularly after the ground has been exposed to changing moisture conditions, repeated loading and seasonal temperature changes.

Pouring Over Loose Or Unprepared Ground

If the concrete is placed directly over loose soil, rubble or poorly compacted fill, the material underneath can settle. That loss of support can leave sections of the slab unsupported and increase the risk of cracking.

Leaving Failed Concrete Underneath

Covering an old, moving patio with fresh concrete doesn't necessarily solve the original problem. We inspect existing sections before deciding whether they can form part of the new construction or need removing.

Incorrect Thickness And Poor Joint Planning

Thin sections, weak edges and poorly positioned joints can leave a patio more vulnerable to damage. We set the slab depth and plan suitable joints before pouring, taking account of the shape and dimensions of the patio.

Rushing The Finish Or Curing

Working the surface while excess bleed water is present or allowing fresh concrete to dry too quickly can affect the finish and increase the risk of surface defects. We manage finishing and curing according to the conditions on the day.

Repairing a cracked or sinking patio can mean breaking out concrete, removing failed ground materials and carrying out the installation again. We would rather identify those risks during preparation than leave a homeowner dealing with them after the garden has been finished.
