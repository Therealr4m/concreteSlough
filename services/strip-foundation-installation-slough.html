Installing Strip Foundations In Slough

Installing Strip Foundations In Slough

Our team at concrete bases slough install strip foundations When a heavier building needs more than a simple surface slab.

The work starts below the finished ground level.
We excavate the foundation runs, deal with uneven or previously disturbed ground and prepare a suitable strip foundation for your building.

This gives the building a proper working foundation rather than putting concrete over whatever ground happens to be there. For heavier buildings when the foundation will be holding a heavier building that preparation can make a major difference to how well it performs over time.

Long-term performance

A Proper Foundation For A Heavier Garden Structure

A heavier garden building puts considerably more demand through it's supporting points than a lightweight garden shed. We therefore look at what is actually being built rather than treating every garden foundation as the same job.

The strip foundations provide defined supporting runs for the structure, helping transfer its load into properly prepared ground. Where reinforcement or additional concrete strength is appropriate, we factor that into the installation rather than adding it as an afterthought.

We also consider finished levels and drainage. The foundation needs to work with the surrounding garden so surface water is not directed towards the building or left sitting against the finished structure.

Typical garden structures we may install foundations for

  • Heavier timber garden offices
  • Large summerhouses and garden cabins
  • Workshops and hobby buildings
  • Substantial garden storage buildings
  • Other heavier residential garden structures
The actual installation

What Strip Foundation Installation Involves

We begin by setting out the foundation runs and establishing the required levels. On uneven ground, this is particularly important because the excavation and finished foundation need to work with the existing levels rather than simply following the surface of the garden.

Where the ground has previously been disturbed, we assess what we are working with before deciding how the foundation should be prepared. Loose fill, old landscaping or inconsistent ground can behave differently once a building starts putting load onto it.

The foundation trenches are then excavated to the required dimensions and the formation is prepared. Depending on the ground and project, this can involve compacted material, reinforcement and other preparation before the concrete is placed.

For the concrete itself, we arrange the appropriate supply for the job. That may involve a ready-mix delivery or coordination with a batching plant, depending on the volume, access and location of the foundations.

Once the concrete is placed, we work to the required levels and leave the foundation properly formed and ready for the next stage of construction.

Ground and access considerations

Uneven Ground And Restricted Access In Slough

Not every Slough garden gives us a straightforward route from the road to the work area. Where access is restricted, we plan the movement of excavation material, aggregate and concrete before the installation starts.

A narrow side passage, rear garden access or limited vehicle access does not automatically prevent a strip foundation installation. It simply means the delivery and handling method needs to be worked out properly rather than relying on a concrete wagon getting directly to the excavation.

Where a full-size concrete delivery cannot reach the foundations, we assess the practical options for getting the concrete from the delivery point to the work area while keeping the pour organised.

Uneven or previously disturbed ground also needs attention before concrete arrives. We do not use the finished concrete to hide poor preparation underneath it. The foundation has to be supported by ground that has been properly assessed and prepared.

Things we check before the pour

  • Foundation position and finished levels
  • Existing ground conditions and disturbed areas
  • Drainage and the direction of surface water
  • Access for excavation and concrete delivery
  • Concrete quantity and practical delivery method
  • Whether reinforcement or additional preparation is required
Why proper preparation matters

Why We Put So Much Attention Into The Ground

The visible part of a foundation is the concrete. The part underneath it is what determines whether that concrete has a reliable platform to work from.

We regularly see situations where the temptation is to get the concrete down quickly without properly dealing with the ground first. That can save time on the day but create considerably bigger problems once a heavy structure has been installed.

Our approach is straightforward: establish what the building needs, prepare the ground accordingly, organise the access and then place the concrete. It keeps the installation controlled from the start.

We also keep communication direct. If access changes the way materials need to be brought in, or the ground requires additional preparation, we explain what needs doing rather than leaving the issue until the concrete is arriving.

What our approach is designed to achieve

  • Ground prepared before the foundation is poured
  • Foundation runs set out accurately for the structure
  • Finished levels considered alongside drainage
  • Concrete supply organised around the actual access available
  • A solid working foundation ready for the next construction stage
Avoiding expensive problems

What Happens When The Foundation Is Not Prepared Properly?

The main problem we are trying to prevent is movement. If a foundation is sitting on inconsistent, loose or poorly prepared ground, different sections can settle differently once the weight of the building is transferred onto them.

That movement can leave the structure uneven. Doors can become difficult to operate, timber joints can move and the building can end up carrying loads differently from how it was intended.

Cracking can also occur where the foundation or supporting ground has not been dealt with properly. Drainage problems can make matters worse if water is repeatedly allowed to collect around the foundation or finished building.

This is why we concentrate on preparation before the concrete arrives. Once the building is sitting on the foundation, correcting movement underneath it is far more disruptive than preparing the ground properly in the first place.

Our installation process

How We Carry Out A Strip Foundation Installation

1. Site visit and assessment

We look at the proposed building, garden levels, ground conditions and access. This is also where we can identify any practical issue that could affect excavation or concrete delivery.

2. Set out and excavation

The foundation runs are marked out and excavated to the required layout and levels. Uneven ground is accounted for rather than allowing the existing surface to dictate the finished foundation.

3. Ground preparation

The trench formation is checked and prepared before concrete is ordered. Where required, suitable sub-base material, compaction and reinforcement are incorporated into the preparation.

4. Concrete delivery and placement

We coordinate the concrete supply around the quantity required and the access available. Where the wagon cannot reach the work area directly, we plan how the concrete will be moved and placed before the pour starts.

5. Levels, finishing and handover

The concrete is placed to the required levels and the foundation is finished ready for the next stage. We leave the site organised so you know what has been completed and what needs to happen next.

Built around the structure

A Foundation Designed Around What You Are Actually Building

A strip foundation is not simply a trench filled with concrete. Its position, dimensions, supporting ground, levels and relationship with the building all need to make sense together.

For a heavier garden structure, we want the foundation to provide a dependable working platform for the construction that follows. That means looking at the project as a whole rather than treating the concrete as an isolated job.

If you already have building plans or specifications, bring them to the site visit. They help us understand where the supporting walls or loads are intended to sit and how the foundation needs to be prepared.
