Garden concrete installation

Professional Slough Concrete Steps and Walkway Installation

Ground preparation & installation

How We Install Concrete Garden Steps and Walkways

Before starting a concrete walkway or set of garden steps, we check the route, measure the changes in ground height and establish where the finished concrete needs to sit. This is particularly important when the new installation has to meet existing paving, a patio or a garden building entrance.

Once the layout is agreed, we organise the groundwork and concrete installation around the site conditions. On smaller residential jobs, this often means working within narrow garden spaces and carefully moving excavated material and concrete through restricted access points.

Our installation process

  • Site assessment and setting out: We measure the proposed walkway, check ground levels and establish the position, width and height of any steps.
  • Excavation and ground preparation: We remove turf, loose soil and unsuitable material to reach a suitable formation level for the new concrete installation.
  • Sub-base installation: Where required, we install and compact suitable granular sub-base material to provide firm, consistent support beneath the concrete.
  • Formwork and step construction: We set out and secure the shuttering, checking step dimensions, finished heights and the required walkway falls before pouring.
  • Concrete placement: We arrange an appropriate concrete supply for the job, using ready-mixed concrete where practical or another suitable supply method for smaller, restricted-access installations.
  • Finishing and curing: We finish the concrete to the agreed surface, check the edges and levels, and allow suitable curing time before the steps or walkway are brought into use.

Where the design or ground conditions call for it, we also consider reinforcement, appropriate joints and additional support. These details are assessed for the particular installation rather than treating every garden path or set of steps the same way.

Sloping gardens & restricted access

Installing Concrete Steps and Walkways on Uneven Ground

Sloping gardens need more planning than a straightforward walkway across flat ground. When we install concrete steps in a garden with changing levels, we work out the height difference first and establish a practical arrangement of steps and landings.

We aim for consistent step rises and suitable tread dimensions, so the finished route feels natural to use. Where the ground falls away, we may need to excavate higher sections, build up and compact suitable material in lower areas, or form properly supported changes in level.

We also check how rainwater will move around the new concrete. A walkway needs suitable falls to shed surface water, while steps and landings must be arranged to avoid creating areas where water collects against the property or surrounding structures.

Working in tight Slough gardens

Restricted garden access is something we account for before arranging materials. If the only route is through a narrow side passage or between existing landscaping, we plan how excavation waste, sub-base and concrete will be moved without unnecessarily disturbing the surrounding garden.

For larger pours, we check whether ready-mixed concrete can be delivered close enough to the working area. Where direct access isn't possible, we assess practical alternatives such as controlled barrow runs or suitable pumping arrangements.

Getting the excavation, access and material supply organised beforehand helps keep the installation controlled once the concrete work begins.

Why choose our installation approach

Why Getting Concrete Step and Walkway Levels Right Makes All the Difference

One of the first things we establish on a concrete steps or walkway installation is the finished level. It's a detail that affects almost everything else, from how comfortably the steps can be used to how the concrete connects with the rest of the garden.

If a walkway finishes too high, it can interfere with existing paving, door thresholds or drainage. If it finishes too low, it can create awkward transitions and areas where surface water collects. With steps, inaccurate setting out can leave inconsistent rises that make the finished installation uncomfortable or unsafe to use.

We check these details before the concrete is placed. That means working from agreed reference heights, securing the formwork properly and making sure the ground underneath has been prepared to support the finished installation.

What we focus on during your installation

  • Correct finished heights: Steps and walkways positioned to connect properly with existing garden features.
  • Consistent step dimensions: Carefully set out rises and treads for practical everyday access.
  • Proper ground support: Excavation and sub-base preparation suited to the ground and intended use.
  • Water management: Appropriate walkway falls and drainage considerations built into the layout.
  • Organised installation: Clear planning for excavation, concrete delivery, access and finishing.

We keep communication straightforward throughout the job. If existing ground levels, access restrictions or drainage arrangements affect the proposed installation, we'll explain what needs to be considered before work proceeds.

Our approach is to get the preparation right rather than rush straight into pouring concrete. Problems underneath a finished walkway or set of steps are much harder and more expensive to correct once everything has hardened.

Avoiding installation problems

Preventing Sinking, Cracking and Incorrect Concrete Levels

When we're asked to look at an existing concrete path that's started sinking or cracking, the problem isn't always the concrete itself. Poorly compacted ground, inadequate sub-base preparation and unsupported edges can all contribute to movement underneath the finished surface.

Incorrect levels can cause equally frustrating problems. A walkway that directs rainwater towards a building, or steps that don't line up properly with a patio, can leave the homeowner needing alterations after the rest of the garden has already been completed.

Problems we work to avoid

  • Uneven settlement: Sections of concrete dropping where the ground or underlying fill hasn't been properly prepared.
  • Cracking and movement: Concrete affected by inadequate support, restraint, jointing or ground movement.
  • Inconsistent step heights: Poorly measured rises creating awkward or potentially hazardous access.
  • Standing water: Incorrect falls allowing rainwater to collect on walkways or around steps.
  • Poor connections: Finished concrete sitting too high or low against patios, paving and entrances.

Concrete can still develop cracks or experience movement over time, but correct preparation, suitable detailing and controlled installation help reduce avoidable problems.

That's why we pay attention to the ground and finished levels before the pour. Once concrete steps are formed and hardened, correcting their height or position is rarely a simple adjustment.

Planning your concrete installation

What We Check Before Starting Your Concrete Steps or Walkway

When arranging a site visit in Slough, we'll look at the proposed route, the condition of the existing ground and how the finished concrete needs to connect with surrounding surfaces.

We'll also check the working space and access for materials. This helps us understand whether the job can be completed with straightforward excavation and concrete placement or whether additional preparation is required.

During the site assessment, we consider:

  • The overall length, width and intended use of the walkway
  • The total height difference and required step arrangement
  • Existing soil conditions and the amount of excavation needed
  • Sub-base depth, compaction and support requirements
  • Drainage, surface falls and connections to existing paving
  • Restricted access, material handling and concrete supply
  • Suitable surface finishes, step edges and slip resistance

For steps serving a building entrance or where a substantial change in ground level needs supporting, we'll also consider whether additional structural detailing or applicable building requirements need to be addressed.

Once we've seen the site and understood what you're planning, we can discuss the installation approach and provide an estimate based on the actual work involved.
