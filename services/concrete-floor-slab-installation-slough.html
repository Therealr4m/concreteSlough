Concrete Floor Slab Installations In Slough

We carry out concrete floor slab installations throughout Slough, preparing and pouring solid, level slabs for new garden sheds and outdoor buildings. Our work covers the complete installation, from removing unsuitable ground and building a compacted sub-base to setting the levels and finishing the concrete.

Most homeowners contacting us already have a shed ordered or know the size of the building they want. What they need is a concrete slab installed to the correct dimensions, on ground that has been properly prepared, so the building can be fitted without problems.

We work from the requirements of the structure going on top. That means checking the proposed position, the condition of the ground and the finished height before deciding how the slab needs to be constructed.

What our concrete slab installation includes

  • Setting out the slab to suit the shed dimensions and installation requirements
  • Excavating and removing unsuitable surface material
  • Preparing and compacting a suitable sub-base
  • Installing accurate formwork and checking finished levels
  • Pouring and finishing the concrete to the required specification
  • Planning access, material deliveries and the concrete pour

The aim is a properly constructed working base that supports the building, rather than simply a finished-looking slab of concrete.

Typical installation requirements

Concrete Slabs For New Garden Sheds In Slough

A common job for us is preparing a concrete floor slab for a small garden shed where the homeowner has already chosen the building but the garden is not ready for installation.

The proposed area might be uneven lawn, an old flower bed or a section of garden that has previously been dug over. We assess what is underneath before preparing the footprint, rather than pouring concrete directly onto whatever surface is already there.

New shed going onto an existing lawn

Where the shed is being installed on grass, we mark out the required footprint and excavate through the turf and topsoil to reach suitable supporting ground. We then prepare the sub-base and establish the finished slab height.

We also check how the new slab will sit against the surrounding lawn so rainwater does not collect around the shed or run towards its base.

Concrete slab on uneven garden ground

Uneven ground needs more than a quick scrape across the surface. We establish the correct finished level first, then adjust the excavation and preparation underneath to provide consistent support.

We do not simply follow the slope of the garden with the concrete. The shed needs a level bearing surface, even when the surrounding ground falls away.

Previously disturbed or filled ground

Some gardens have areas that were previously used for planting, landscaping or old structures. These sections can contain loose soil, buried rubble or poorly compacted material.

We check the condition of the ground during preparation and remove or address unsuitable material before building the slab. Where deeper fill or particularly weak ground is found, we assess whether additional excavation or a different construction approach is needed.

Shed installation with limited garden access

Many Slough properties have a side passage, garden gate or paved route leading to the rear garden. We plan how excavation waste, aggregates and concrete will move through the property before starting.

For smaller slabs, moving materials through standard garden access is often straightforward with the right preparation. We organise the work around the available route and protect surrounding areas where practical.

Ground preparation and slab construction

How We Prepare A Concrete Floor Slab That Stays Level

The most important part of a concrete floor slab is often the work that disappears underneath it. Once the concrete has been poured, the quality of the excavation and sub-base preparation is no longer visible.

We concentrate on getting that preparation right because a strong concrete slab still needs stable ground supporting it.

Excavation to suitable supporting ground

We start by checking the proposed slab position and excavating the required area. Turf, loose topsoil, roots and unsuitable material are removed so the base is not relying on soft ground that can compress later.

The excavation depth depends on the ground conditions, the intended sub-base and the concrete specification. We establish these requirements before setting the formwork.

Installing and compacting the sub-base

Once the ground is prepared, we install suitable granular sub-base material, commonly MOT Type 1 where appropriate, and compact it thoroughly using mechanical compaction equipment.

Where the required depth makes it necessary, the material is placed and compacted in layers. This helps provide consistent support across the slab rather than leaving loose sections underneath.

Setting accurate levels and formwork

We set the formwork to the required dimensions and check the levels before the concrete arrives. The slab needs to be square, correctly positioned and suitable for the shed manufacturer's base requirements.

Getting the levels right at this stage makes the building installation much easier. Shed panels, doors and the floor structure should not have to compensate for an uneven concrete surface.

Concrete thickness and reinforcement

We determine the slab thickness from the size and intended use of the building, expected loading and supporting ground conditions. A small storage shed will not necessarily need the same slab specification as a heavier workshop.

Where reinforcement is appropriate, we install it with suitable support and cover so it sits in the intended position within the concrete rather than directly on the sub-base.

We establish the slab specification around the actual building and site conditions, rather than applying the same depth and construction method to every garden.

Practical installation details

Drainage, Finished Height And Garden Access

Before pouring a concrete slab, we also look at how it will sit within the rest of the garden. A base can be perfectly level and still cause problems if its finished height or surrounding drainage has not been considered.

Keeping surface water away from the shed

We assess the natural fall of the garden and where rainwater currently travels. Where practical, we position the finished slab so surrounding ground and paving do not direct water towards the building.

For a shed requiring a level base, we maintain the required level bearing surface and deal with drainage through the surrounding ground levels or suitable drainage arrangements. We do not introduce a fall across the slab that could interfere with the shed installation.
