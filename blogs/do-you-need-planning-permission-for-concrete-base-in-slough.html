Planning Guidance for Slough Homeowners

Do You Need Planning Permission for a Concrete Shed Base in Slough?

In most standard situations in Slough, the answer is no however there are important exceptions. The actual base itself is rarely the issue. It’s what’s going on top of it where it will be positioned and how large the structure is.
They are usually the questions that determines whether permission is required or not.

Having installed concrete bases across Slough we’ve seen a mix of straightforward jobs and a few where homeowners were surprised by restrictions. So it’s worth understanding the basics before any work starts.

The Short Answer for Most Gardens

If you're installing a concrete base for a typical garden shed or workshop in a standard residential property, it normally falls under “permitted development”.

Permitted development rights allow homeowners to build certain structures without applying for full planning permission, provided they meet size and location limits.

You can read the official guidance directly on the UK Government website here: Permitted development rights for householders (gov.uk).

In simple terms, most garden outbuildings do not need planning permission if:

  • The structure is single storey
  • It does not take up more than 50% of your garden area
  • It is not positioned forward of the main front wall of the house
  • It stays within height limits (especially near boundaries)

The concrete base itself is considered groundwork supporting that structure. So if the building complies, the base usually does too.

When Planning Permission Might Be Required

Depending on what type of building is going on top of your base there are situations where permission is necessary.

We’ve come across this particularly with:

  • Large log cabins being used as offices or gyms
  • Outbuildings very close to neighbouring boundaries
  • Properties in conservation areas
  • Flats or maisonettes without full permitted development rights

In older parts of Slough and near certain heritage areas, restrictions can be tighter.

What Can Surprise Homeowners With Planning Permission

The biggest surprise is that planning permission and building regulations are not the same thing.

Small detached garden sheds generally don’t require building regulation approval unless they are used for sleeping accommodation or include plumbing or electrics that fall under other regulations.

Another surprise is boundary height. If your shed sits within 2 metres of a boundary, height limits are stricter. That can affect how thick we'd build the base and whether a raised base is suitable.

Access is another real-world factor. Many Slough gardens have side returns or narrow paths. If excavation equipment can’t get through, groundwork becomes manual. That doesn’t affect planning, but it does affect preparation time and cost.

FAQ

Do I need planning permission just for the concrete slab?

In most cases, no. The slab supports the structure. Planning rules usually relate to the size and position of the shed or outbuilding.

What if my shed will be used as a home office?

Occasional office use is generally fine under permitted development. Permanent residential use would require further checks.

Do neighbours need to be notified?

Not for permitted development. However, it’s always good practice to inform close neighbours if access or noise may temporarily affect them.

Will you advise if permission looks necessary?

Yes. If something appears outside normal limits, we’ll mention it before any groundwork begins.
